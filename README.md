# The-standard-website-development-workflow

1. THE STANDARD WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW 

01
Discovery and requirements:
Understand the client's business, audience, goals, and features. / Understand the business and goals
Project brief: A one-page summary of the business, target audience, goals, required functionality, constraints, and success criteria.
Ask:
What does your business do?
What makes you different from competitors?
What problems are they trying to solve?
What information must the website include?
Do you need a booking system, contact forms, payments, or a blog?
Who are your ideal customers?
What is your target launch date?
Do you already have a logo, photos, text, and brand guidelines?
Are there websites you like? What specifically appeals to you?
Who will provide and approve the content?
Who will maintain the website after launch?

02
Planning and site structure:
Define the sitemap, navigation, content, and functionality. / Agree on pages, navigation, content, and functionality
Sitemap: A diagram or list showing the pages and how visitors will move between them.

03
Design and approval:
Create wireframes or mockups and agree on the visual direction. / Show the proposed structure and appearance / Confirm the direction before full development
Homepage wireframe: A simple layout showing the header, hero, main sections, calls to action, and footer without detailed visual styling.
Visual direction: A mood board or style guide with proposed colors, typography, imagery, and examples of the desired look and feel.

04
Development:
Build the website using WordPress, themes, blocks, plugins, and code. / Build

05
Testing and quality assurance:
Check responsiveness, links, forms, accessibility, SEO, and performance.

06
Client review:
Collect feedback, fix issues, and obtain final approval. /  Review, revise within the agreed scope, and launch

07
Launch:
Deploy the site, configure the domain, backups, security, and analytics.

08
Maintenance
Update WordPress, monitor performance, resolve issues, and maintain security.


2. WHAT DOES A WORKFLOW LOOK LIKE IN PRACTICE?

Imagine a client hires your team to build a WordPress website for a local business.
Stage	What you do
Requirements	Find out what the client needs and what the site must accomplish.
Planning	Create the sitemap and identify required pages and features.
Design	Establish the layout, typography, colors, and imagery.
Development	Build the approved design in WordPress.
Testing	Test mobile layouts, forms, links, performance, and accessibility.
Review	Let the client review the website and provide feedback.
Launch	Publish the website and verify that everything works.
The workflow also determines who is responsible for each task, how work is reviewed, and how the team handles changes.

3. WHAT ARE INTERNAL WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT PROCESSES AND WORKFLOW

This is especially relevant to your interview.
An internal workflow is how the company organizes the work behind the scenes. For example:
- Project management: How tasks are assigned and tracked.
- Development environments: How developers build and test sites before publishing changes.
- Reusable components: How the team avoids rebuilding the same features repeatedly.
- Code reviews: How developers check each other's code for quality and consistency.
- Quality assurance: How the team tests websites before delivery.
- Documentation: How the team records instructions and project decisions.
- Deployment: How changes move safely from development to production.
These processes help prevent mistakes, reduce duplicated effort, and make it easier for team members to collaborate.

4. HOW COULD YOU IMPROVE A WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW?

   Reuse existing components
Create standard hero sections, buttons, card layouts, headers, and footers using Gutenberg or Kadence blocks.
Result: Less repetitive work.


Use a standard launch checklist
Make sure every website receives the same essential checks before going live.
Result: Fewer missed issues.


Use AI to assist development
Use AI tools to draft code, troubleshoot errors, explain unfamiliar code, and help review accessibility or consistency.
Result: Potential time savings, with developer review and testing.


Standardize project tasks
Use a shared project board with clear task owners, deadlines, and approval stages.
Result: Better visibility and fewer communication gaps.

CLIENT DISCOVERY WORKSHEET:
- What does the business do? Describe its services, products, or mission.
- Who does it serve?/ Who is the target audience? Describe the ideal customer or visitor.
- What is the primary goal of the website? Generate leads / Sell products / Provide information / Showcase work / Increase bookings
- What problem should the website solve? Example: Visitors can not find services or contact the business easily.
- What should the website feel like? Professional and approachable/ Modern and minimal / Premium and elegant / Warm and community-focused / Bold and creative
- What features or pages are required? Services, About us, Portfolio, Forms, Online store, Booking, etc
- Any reference websites? URLs or descriptions of the sites the client likes.
